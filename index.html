Foundations, architectures, and applications

What the tutorial covers

Deep networks are very good at local and pairwise structure. Topology gives us tools for the rest: the shape, connectivity, and higher-order organisation of data and of the representations a model learns.

Standard models can miss global structure, weak signals, and the properties of data that stay the same under noise, sampling variation, or domain shift. Topological methods measure exactly those properties, and over the past few years they have moved from an analysis tool applied after training to something that can sit inside the learning pipeline.

This tutorial introduces the foundations and current practice of topological deep learning. We start from persistent homology, filtrations, and persistence diagrams, then show how topological summaries become features, losses, and differentiable layers. From there we move to neural networks defined on graphs, simplicial and cellular complexes, hypergraphs, and sheaves.

We also spend time on the practical questions: when topology actually helps, how to combine it with a deep network, how to show that it contributed, and what still does not work. Examples come from visual recognition, hyperspectral imaging, biomedical analysis, and representation learning with foundation models.

By the end, you will be able to

  • Interpret persistence diagrams and related topological summaries.
  • Identify machine learning settings where topology is likely to be useful.
  • Compare topology-aware features, losses, layers, and architectures.
  • Design appropriate baselines and ablations for topology-based methods.

Who it is for

Researchers, students, and practitioners with a general machine learning background. You do not need to have studied algebraic topology or topological data analysis. The emphasis is on intuition and computational meaning, not formal proof.

    Motivation: why topology in machine learning?

    Local distances and pairwise graphs do not always capture global structure, holes, connectedness, representation collapse, domain shift, or weak signals. We introduce topology as a complementary tool for analysing and improving learned representations.

Where topology earns its place

The longest part of the session looks at problems where connectivity and shape carry information that pixel-level or pairwise objectives miss.

Vision and medical imaging

Vessels, roads, and cell boundaries must stay connected. Topology-aware losses penalise a broken branch that barely changes pixel accuracy.

Hyperspectral and remote sensing

Hundreds of bands, few labels, and faint targets. Topological summaries help separate weak but persistent signals from noise.

Anomaly detection and robustness

Anomalies and distribution shift change the shape of a representation. Topology and geometry give diagnostics for detecting that change.

Molecular and scientific data

Rings, cavities, and functional groups are higher-order structures. Cell-complex and simplicial networks represent them directly.
